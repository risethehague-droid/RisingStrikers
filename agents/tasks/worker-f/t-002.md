## How it works now (check it yourself)
- **Controls:** LOB is C (arrows) or L (WASD). A tap is a lob to a team-mate; holding it charges a power lob
  (`lobCharge` 0 → 1 over 0.9 s): `actions.js` around line 65, `ball.js` `powerLob` / `lobSpot` around line 202.
- **The chip:** LOB in their box while facing the goal (`chipCan` → `chipShot`, `ball.js` around line 250). It flies
  fast and low, then dips hard after its peak (`CHIP_DIP` in `core.js`). Facing away it's a normal lob.
- **The computer:** chips a keeper who has come out, by difficulty (`teamai.js` around line 310, knob `chipOff`).
  It also picks lob passes (`teamai.js` around lines 356 and 397).
- **The keeper:** goes BACK for a lob over him (`keeper.js`); goalkeepers throw or punt with `mode: 'lob'`.
- **Also uses the lob:** set pieces (`matchflow.js`) and the aerial specials off a lob (`specials.js`). These must
  keep working; change them only if your direction needs it, and say so.
- **Art:** the chip animation is PixelLab batch 15. `kit_build.py` maps it as `chip`.

## 1. Research (with sources)
- **Real ball flight of lobs and chips:** drag, backspin and lift (Magnus effect), why a chip rises steeply and
  drops, typical speeds, heights and flight times.
- **How football games handle the lob and the chip,** in words only: controls (FC / FIFA's chip shot, lob pass and
  driven lob, PES / eFootball, older arcade games), how power and height are chosen, and how the player reads the
  arc (shadow, landing marker, camera).
- **What makes it feel good:** readable arc, anticipation, timing, keeper reaction, sound, and the ball's shadow.
