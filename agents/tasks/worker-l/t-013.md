**1. The net has give** (Test Lab only for now)
- A ball into the net doesn't stop dead: the net catches it like a spring - it bulges back, slows the ball over a short
  distance, holds it a beat and lets it drop; a hard shot ripples it more (and the side netting / roof too).
- The look: move and bend the existing net art (`goal-*-net_back` / `net_near` from the props atlas) - strips or a
  mesh, displaced where the ball hits - never draw new art in code (CLAUDE.md "Art").
- Research how football games handle net physics and say what you based yours on (words only in the PR).

**2. Posts and bar with real rebounds**
- Hitting the woodwork uses the ball's real height, speed and where it meets the round post / bar (the share of the
  post it catches): a glancing touch carries on (in or wide), a square hit bounces back with energy loss and a bit of
  spin, a ball under the bar can drop down onto the line and bounce out or in.
- With enough power and a square hit, the ball comes back into play around the six-yard box / the edge of the box, at a
  height a player can meet: a header, a volley, the start of an aerial duel or an aerial special. Moderate: most
  woodwork hits still go out or get cleared; no ping-pong loops.

**3. The keeper's guard broken -> the ball pops up**
- When the keeper gets to a shot but can't hold it (a parry, or a special that breaks his guard), more of those go UP
  - a looping deflection a few metres off him, sometimes back into the box - so an attacker following in can pounce.
  Not every time, and less on Easy for the computer's keeper vs you (fair to both sides; check the levels).
