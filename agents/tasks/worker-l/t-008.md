**1. Hold to aim, release to kick** (your lob, open play)
- Today: LOB held charges `lobCharge` 0 -> 1 in 0.9 s and the landing distance grows with it (`lobSpot`): too fast,
  and you can't place it.
- New: pressing LOB puts a **landing ring** on the grass where a lob would go right now (the team-mate the pass logic
  would pick in that direction, or ~20 m ahead). While LOB is held, the stick / keys **move the ring** at a steady,
  controllable speed (one number, start ~16 m/s; a short ease-in so a tap of the key nudges it), inside a sensible
  range (about 8-45 m from the kicker, on the pitch). **Release = the kick**: the launch is solved so the ball comes
  down on the ring (use the real flight, `lobPreview`, so it lands where the ring said, within ~1 m).
- The arc comes from the distance and what's in the way: higher over a defender in the path, flatter / driven when the
  lane is clear and it's short. Players hold the button a bit longer for a higher, softer ball if that feels natural
  (your call; say what you chose).
- A light magnet: within ~3 m of a team-mate the ring settles on the spot he'll reach (he starts moving there).
- A quick tap (under ~0.18 s) stays the instant lob to a team-mate. The chip in their box (`chipCan` / `chipShot`)
  stays as it is. Set pieces keep their own aim cursor.

**2. Receiving: smooth, so aerial shots happen more**
- The team-mate the ring is on starts his run as soon as you release, timed to arrive under the drop (not stand and
  wait); his marker reacts a beat later.
- At the drop: the existing header / volley / chest-trap / overhead choices (`actions.js` headers, the aerial special
  on SPECIAL) with a window you can hit; when you press SHOOT as it drops, the strike goes towards goal.
- Coordinate with t-012 (wing play): it changes the computer's crosses and runs in `teamai.js`; keep your receiving
  run in its own small function (or merge t-012's branch first, below) so they fit.

**3. The AERIAL BATTLE** (your lob arriving between your man and a defender, and the computer's lob arriving between
its man and you)
- Trigger: the ball's drop point is contested: an attacker and a defender can both reach it at the drop (within about
  2 m of each other and of the spot). Not on every lob: only clear contests.
- A smooth transition: a short slow-motion / freeze as both rise, the camera tightens a little, **MASH!** (or PRESS!)
  on screen, sparks and impacts from the existing PixelLab `fx` atlas, the crowd rising, a sound per press.
- You mash SHOOT (and the pad's shoot button) for ~1.2 s; the computer mashes at a rate set by the level (Easy slow,
  Rise Warrior fast, a little random), plus a head start for the better position (closer to the drop, facing it,
  taller jump). A bar or tug-of-war shows who's winning, live.
- Attacker wins: he meets it (header / volley towards goal, or the aerial special if SP is banked). Defender wins:
  it's cleared, half random and half by position (away from his goal, towards the nearer touchline, never straight
  back into the middle of his own box).
- Fair to both sides; not every drop; a quick pass-through when the camera / pace would make it annoying (e.g. not
  twice in 5 s).

All numbers in one place (core.js) so Luca can tune them in the Lab. No new art: the ring and bars are code-drawn HUD
markers like the existing ones; effects come from the `fx` atlas.
