**1. Tap vs hold**
- Today SHOOT held charges `charge` 0 -> 1 in 0.8 s and the release shoots (`doShot`), so a quick press is just a weak
  power shot.
- New: a **tap** (released within ~0.15 s, one number) is the tap kick; holding past that is the power shot exactly as
  today. Set pieces, the chip, specials and volleys / headers keep their own rules.

**2. The tap kick**
- Snappy and predictable: low, quick off the boot (about 55-70 km/h), straight along the direction you point (the
  t-009 pointer, or the facing when you don't push), almost no error, no curve; it **drops off** fast (grips the
  grass / loses pace) so it's a close-range finish, useless from 25 m.
- The keeper: inside his reach it's easy for him (he holds most of them: a tapped shot at a set keeper is a gift);
  out of his reach (he's committed, wrong-footed, or a cut-back finds you with him on the other post) it goes in.
  Make his "reach" honest: his position, his set / moving state and the ball's travel time, through t-007's save
  logic, not a dice.
- The animation: the PixelLab `tap` move (PR #12, made for this), E / W; contact on its strike frame.
- A short sharp sound and a small impact (existing fx), lighter than a power shot.
